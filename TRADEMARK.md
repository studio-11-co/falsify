# Trademark policy

This file states what is and is not covered by the licences in this repository,
and what the current legal status of the marks actually is. It exists because a
licence to copy a specification is not a licence to use the name on the front of
it, and implementers' counsel ask about exactly this.

## The marks

- **FALSIFY** — word mark.
- **⟐ falsify** — the chevron mark.
- **PRML** and **Pre-Registered ML Manifest** — used as the name of the
  specification.

Asserted by **Falsify OÜ** (Estonia, registry code 17574308).

## Current status — stated plainly

**These marks are not registered anywhere.** They are unregistered marks, used
in commerce and asserted with ™. No application has been filed with EUIPO, the
Estonian Patent Office, the USPTO or any other office as of 2026-08-29.

That has two consequences worth knowing before you rely on anything here:

1. Our ability to stop a third party using the name rests on unregistered-mark
   protection, which is narrower and harder to enforce than a registration.
2. Anyone evaluating a transfer of these marks — for example a foundation that
   requires marks to be assigned as a condition of hosting a project — should
   know they would be receiving an unregistered mark.

Earlier notices in this repository asserted the marks in the founder's personal
name; the project has been operated by Falsify OÜ since the company was
incorporated on 2026-08-12, and the marks are asserted by the company.

## What the licences do and do not grant

- The **MIT License** covers the source code. It grants no rights in the marks.
- The **Community Specification License 1.0** covers the specification. Its
  Section 1.2 *requires* attribution to the Working Group, naming the material,
  its version number and where it was retrieved from — that is an attribution
  obligation, not a trademark licence.

Neither licence permits use of the FALSIFY name or chevron mark in connection
with a competing or derivative product, a commercial service, or marketing and
promotional material, without prior written permission.

## Uses that need no permission

You may, without asking us:

- State truthfully that your product **implements PRML**, is **compatible with
  PRML**, or **passes the PRML conformance vectors**. Nominative use of the
  specification's name to describe what your software does is expected and
  welcome.
- Cite and quote the specification, with the attribution the Community
  Specification License requires.
- Name `falsify` when referring to this project — in documentation, papers,
  comparisons, talks, package dependency lists and changelogs.
- Use the marks in the ordinary course of reporting, review or criticism.

## Uses that need permission

- Naming your own product, service, company or package `falsify`, or a name
  confusingly similar to it.
- Using the chevron mark in your own branding, or as a badge implying we
  endorse, certify or approve your product.
- Implying that a fork or modified version is the official one.

**Forks and derivatives must use a distinct name and a distinct mark.** This is
the normal outcome and it is not a hostile one: the format, the vectors and the
implementations are all yours to continue under their licences. Only the name
stays with us.

## No certification

We do not operate a certification programme, and no mark in this repository is a
certification mark. Nothing published here — including a registry receipt or a
passing conformance run — means that Falsify OÜ has certified, approved or
endorsed any product, model or evaluation result. PRML records that an
evaluation's bar was fixed before the run; it makes no assertion that the
result is correct.
